# Social trust and social tension

### Social trust
Social trust is the **glue** that holds a society together. If it is gone, or fraying, watch out: societies have been known to first unravel and then disappear. Can you think of an example?

In the model trust is a function of four influences:

- income inequality: The more unequal income is distributed, the more trust erodes,
- public spending: The higher public spending on schools, health care, public transport, etc., the more trust is built-up,
- years of schooling: The longer folks go to school, the more trust is being built-up, (any idea why that may be so?)
- gender inequality: The more equal people (men, women and others) are, the more **all** people have equal rights, the more trust is being built-up. (Again, can you think of why this may be so?)

Trust is a slow moving animal, it takes years before changes in the input make a difference. True to the saying attributed to President Abraham Lincoln: "You can fool *some* of the people *all* the time. You can fool *all* of the people *some* of the time. But you cannot fool **all** the people **all** of the time." But beware, it can be lost quicker than you can say: "But I thought you loved me ..."

### Social tension
Tension is a shorter-term effect. Ranging from grumbling, to striking, to demonstrations, to sporadic violence, to revolutions.

In the model, it depends on

- social trust (see above): the more trust, the lower the tension,
- wealth inequality: the more the rich and really rich have stashed away, the higher the tension, and
- the development of your living conditions over time: It is the famous question of any opposition politician running for office: "Are you better off today than you were 4 years ago?" If things are getting better, people are content and tension is low, but if things are worse than they were in the past, people are **dis**content and tensions rise.

#### Living conditions
Those are made up of the following:

- environmental damage, like extreme weather, dams breaking, heat waves, dry spells, etc.,
- whether you have a job or not,
- whether you have enough electricity or not,
- whether you have enough to eat or not,
- how high the public spending is (for example, if your monthly bus ticket is (partly) subsidized or if you and your family have to pay the full cost),
- if your income is enough for a decent life,
- if you get your fair share of wages.

### Coupled but lagged
Note that trust and tension are connected, but the connection is **not** instantaneous, it is lagged. So you may see both going up, or down, at the same time, even though this is quite contradictory (Why?).
